# WIND — Worldbuilding In Narrative Design

WIND is a method for developing an IP through scenes that make its design unavoidable. It converts an abstract subject into a situational spread: one cinematic event supported by evidence extracted from that event.

## Recipe-engine dependency

Before running WIND, invoke `$signal-recipe-engine` and read [the publishing pipeline profile](../signal-recipe-engine/references/publishing-pipeline.md). WIND normally selects **Situation-Forced Design** as its primary recipe. Add **Sequential Reveal** when the request is a connected panel/page sequence, and add **Constraint-Bearing System** when a power, ritual, institution, or economy must be learned through operation and cost.

For the witches IP or any other graphic-novel MVP harness, do not treat “extend the lore” as permission to write detached encyclopedia material. Select the chapter/spread recipe first, make the lore operate in a scene, then extract only the canon needed by the panels.

WIND preserves the narrative architecture. The visual skin belongs to the current project and must be invented, inferred, or explicitly selected; never inherit another project's style by default.

## Operating rules

1. **Situation before sheet.** Find the event that proves what the subject is. Do not default to a lineup, front/side/back sheet, isolated render, inventory grid, or lore dump.
2. **Hero image first.** Make one dominant image carry the idea before annotations do. It should show subject, setting, scale, action, relationship, consequence, and at least one unanswered question.
3. **Design through consequence.** Derive appearance, tools, clothing, architecture, behavior, hierarchy, biology, economics, and rules from what the situation requires.
4. **Evidence panels, not repetition.** Use only supporting panels that extract information from the hero moment: anatomy, behavior, equipment, environment, system, history, observer note, warning, or anomaly.
5. **Show systems operating.** Put a government, economy, technology, ritual, military, or power system under pressure. Prefer input → mechanism → use → cost → failure → aftermath.
6. **Every detail earns its place.** Keep details that explain causality, differentiate the world, reveal character or culture, expose a limitation, establish scale, create pressure, foreshadow conflict, or become reusable canon.
7. **Render style is variable.** Define the current skin's aesthetic thesis, references, materials, typography, color logic, layout density, camera language, realism, cultural influences, and degree of diegetic presentation before rendering.

## Workflow

### 1. Read the request and continuity

Identify the subject, the user's desired depth, whether this is a single spread or a sequence, and what has already been established. Preserve names, rules, relationships, dates, places, motifs, and unresolved mysteries. Treat prior rendered details as continuity evidence.

If the idea is abstract, ask: **What situation would force the audience to understand this simply by watching it happen?** Choose one situation rather than presenting a menu. If a choice is uncertain, make the strongest reasonable choice and label it as a proposal.

### 2. Choose the spread's narrative job

Select the archetype that best advances the idea:

- **Origin:** what made it necessary?
- **Operation:** what does competence or normal function look like?
- **Failure:** what breaks, and what does it cost?
- **Ordinary life:** how has the extraordinary become routine?
- **Economy:** what value, scarcity, or access is moving between people?
- **Political:** which legitimate interests cannot both win?
- **Relationship:** what does interaction reveal that biography would hide?
- **Mystery:** what evidence appears before explanation?
- **Consequence:** what changed because of a prior decision?
- **Reveal:** which assumption is overturned?

Do not make two consecutive spreads do the same primary job.

### 3. Compose the spread

Use this order:

1. **Spread title:** memorable and derived from the event, not merely the subject name.
2. **Narrative job:** what the audience understands differently afterward.
3. **Hero moment:** the exact scene, point of view, action, scale, and visible consequence.
4. **What it secretly designs:** the canon extracted from the scene.
5. **Evidence panels:** only the necessary supporting views or annotations.
6. **Emotional/thematic beat:** why this matters beyond spectacle.
7. **Forward hook:** the pressure, contradiction, or question that makes the next spread feel earned.

The hero image should remain the conceptual center, roughly 50–70% of the spread's emphasis. The viewer should learn something before reading.

### 4. Sequence with IMMERSION and SPINE

For multiple spreads, alternate between:

- **IMMERSION:** daily life, work, markets, rituals, transportation, family, ecology, architecture, or other evidence that makes the world inhabited.
- **SPINE:** discovery, antagonist pressure, betrayal, succession, crisis, transformation, institutional failure, or another beat that advances the core narrative.

A useful progression is:

`Premise → Function → Vulnerability → Immersion → Contrast → Pressure → Relationship → Mystery → Crisis → Transformation`

Adapt it to the IP. The invariant is that each spread must change understanding and the run must contain both lived-world texture and narrative movement.

### 5. Record continuity

After each spread, track new or changed canon under:

`names · dates · places · factions · species · institutions · technologies · rules · costumes · relationships · motifs · mysteries`

Separate **confirmed**, **proposed/inferred**, and **reserved/unknown** information when the project uses a canon registry. Never silently repair a contradiction created by a rendered image; surface it as a continuity decision.

### 6. Return the recipe handoff

For every spread or sequence, include the recipe block from the publishing pipeline profile: primary and secondary recipes, narrative job, controlled mutation, source boundary, output contract, validation, canon status, and unresolved questions. This block travels with the spread into the graphic-novel harness and publishing archive.

## Subject-specific heuristics

### Character

Find what the person wants in the scene, what prevents it, and which decision reveals them. Let that decision force silhouette, clothing, tools, abilities, affiliations, habits, vulnerabilities, and a contradiction into view.

### Faction

Show the group solving, enforcing, celebrating, mourning, negotiating, trading, rescuing, punishing, recruiting, or failing. Extract doctrine, hierarchy, territory, resources, visual language, equipment, allies, enemies, and internal contradiction from behavior under pressure.

### Creature

Treat it as an organism before treating it as a monster. Show feeding, mating, migration, disease, parenting, aging, predation, symbiosis, territory, or interaction with civilization. Extract anatomy, lifecycle, scale, locomotion, senses, ecology, vulnerabilities, and regional variation.

### Technology, power, or system

Show why it exists, who can use it, who cannot afford it, what infrastructure supports it, what misuse looks like, and what failure costs. Never demonstrate only maximum capability.

### Place, institution, or economy

Show it operating on a case that tests its purpose. Let architecture, logistics, class, access, language, ritual, and maintenance emerge from the case rather than from a detached description.

## Style skin protocol

When a project has no established skin, define a compact one before rendering:

`aesthetic thesis · visual references · materials · typography · palette/color logic · layout density · camera/composition · realism · cultural/historical influences · diegetic level`

Keep this skin independent from WIND's structure. The same method may become an evidence-heavy natural-history field record, an occult case file, a luxury editorial archive, a neon street mythology board, a children's sketchbook, a prestige space-opera dossier, or something else entirely. A Paleo-Mythic scientific field-record look is only one example, not the default.

## WIND versus WIND Render

**WIND** means ideate and design the spread. Return the spread plan, hero scene, evidence panels, thematic beat, forward hook, and continuity impact unless the user asks for another format.

**WIND Render** means create the actual visual artifact from an approved or clearly inferable WIND plan. Carry forward the current skin, composition, canon, scale relationships, and evidence hierarchy. If the plan is missing, derive it briefly before rendering; do not jump to an unrelated pretty image.

When a render introduces accidental but compelling canon or contradiction, report it for approval and preserve it in the continuity record only if accepted.

## Command language

- **“WIND this.”** Convert the supplied idea into the strongest situational spread.
- **“WIND [subject].”** Choose the scene that most efficiently reveals the subject.
- **“WIND the faction.”** Show doctrine and internal structure through behavior under pressure.
- **“WIND the cast.”** Introduce characters through meaningful situations, not lineup sheets.
- **“WIND the system.”** Show the system operating, costing, and failing.
- **“WIND the world.”** Build a sequence that moves from premise toward lived civilization.
- **“WIND sequence.”** Plan connected spreads as a visual narrative arc with IMMERSION/SPINE balance.
- **“WIND it. New skin.”** Keep the WIND method and develop a new project-specific art direction.
- **“WIND it cinematically.”** Prioritize composition, scale, action, visual hierarchy, and sequential reveal.
- **“WIND Render.”** Generate the approved visual spread using its current skin and continuity.

## Quality test

Before treating a spread as ready, answer yes to at least eight of these ten checks:

1. Would the hero image communicate something important with all text removed?
2. Does the subject have a reason to be in this exact situation?
3. Does the scene reveal function rather than only appearance?
4. Is consequence visible?
5. Does the design emerge from the world's rules?
6. Is information introduced through need rather than lecture?
7. Does at least one detail create character, culture, or emotional attachment?
8. Is the skin native to this project rather than inherited from another?
9. Did the spread create reusable canon or a meaningful reserved question?
10. Does it create curiosity, pressure, or desire for the next spread?

If it fails, redesign the situation first. Do not solve a weak spread by adding more labels.

## Prime directive

**Do not illustrate the lore. Find the moment in which the lore becomes unavoidable.**
