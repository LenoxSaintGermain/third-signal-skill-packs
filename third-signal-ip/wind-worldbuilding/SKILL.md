# WIND — Worldbuilding In Narrative Design
## Reusable Visual Development Skill v1.0

### PURPOSE

WIND turns abstract worldbuilding, characters, factions, technologies, creatures, institutions, locations, or power systems into **narrative visual-development spreads**.

WIND does not begin with conventional character sheets, encyclopedic descriptions, equipment turnarounds, or exposition.

It asks:

**What situation would force the audience to understand this concept simply by watching it happen?**

That situation becomes the primary design artifact.

Supporting information is then extracted from the event.

---

## CORE DOCTRINE

**Design through consequence.**

Do not show what something looks like in isolation when a scene can demonstrate:

- why it exists,
- what it does,
- what it costs,
- who depends on it,
- what opposes it,
- how people behave around it,
- what culture has formed around it,
- and what story pressure it creates.

The hero image should function as both:

1. a compelling cinematic moment;
2. a compressed explanation of the concept.

---

## WHAT WIND PRESERVES ACROSS PROJECTS

The following belongs to the WIND method and should remain stable:

### 1. Situation Before Sheet

Never default to:

- front / side / rear character poses,
- inventory grids,
- generic faction lineups,
- isolated creature renders,
- detached architecture boards.

Instead identify an event that exposes the design.

Examples:

- healer operating on a creature,
- ruler losing authority during a public dispute,
- technology failing during an emergency,
- antagonist quietly performing an unexpected ordinary act,
- market revealing class inequality,
- military unit responding to a noncombat disaster,
- family ritual revealing history,
- transportation system operating during environmental stress,
- institution handling the exact case it was created to solve.

---

### 2. Hero Image First

Every WIND spread begins with one dominant cinematic composition.

The hero image should typically occupy approximately 50–70% of the conceptual emphasis.

It must contain:

- subject,
- environment,
- scale,
- action,
- consequence,
- relationship,
- visual hierarchy,
- and at least one unanswered question.

The viewer should understand something important before reading any annotation.

---

### 3. Evidence Panels

Supporting panels should not repeat the hero image.

They extract useful design information from it.

Possible panel families include:

**Anatomy**
- body systems,
- physiology,
- damage,
- adaptation,
- comparative scale.

**Behavior**
- rituals,
- tactics,
- movement,
- communication,
- social responses.

**Equipment**
- tools,
- clothing,
- interfaces,
- materials,
- failure states.

**Environment**
- map,
- habitat,
- climate,
- architecture,
- ecological pressures.

**Systems**
- economy,
- governance,
- logistics,
- technology,
- power mechanics,
- rules.

**History**
- timeline,
- inheritance,
- predecessors,
- scars,
- institutional memory.

**Narrative Evidence**
- observer notes,
- dialogue fragment,
- eyewitness sketch,
- warning,
- unresolved anomaly,
- next-record teaser.

Only use panels relevant to the concept being demonstrated.

---

### 4. Every Detail Must Earn Its Place

A detail belongs on the spread only if it does one or more of the following:

- explains causality,
- differentiates the world,
- reveals character,
- creates story pressure,
- exposes a limitation,
- demonstrates culture,
- establishes scale,
- foreshadows conflict,
- or becomes reusable canon.

Avoid decorative lore.

---

### 5. Show Systems Operating

WIND prefers functioning systems over descriptions of systems.

Do not merely describe:

- a government,
- economy,
- religion,
- military,
- school,
- technology,
- magic system,
- transportation network.

Show one operating under pressure.

Examples:

Instead of explaining the court:
**show the court adjudicating a dispute nobody else can solve.**

Instead of explaining the power system:
**show someone using it when failure has consequences.**

Instead of explaining the economy:
**show what rich and poor people can buy differently.**

Instead of explaining a military:
**show what it does when killing is not the objective.**

---

## STYLE IS A VARIABLE, NOT THE FORMAT

WIND must never automatically reuse the visual style of another WIND project.

Before rendering spreads, define or infer the current project's:

- aesthetic thesis,
- visual references,
- materials,
- typography,
- layout density,
- color philosophy,
- camera language,
- level of realism,
- cultural influences,
- interface metaphors,
- historical influences,
- and degree of diegetic presentation.

The same WIND structure might produce:

### Speculative Natural History
Scientific plates, field notes, specimens, maps, observational photography.

### Afrofuturist Luxury Archive
Glossy editorial layouts, materials studies, holographic annotations, fashion photography.

### Occult Bureaucratic Horror
Redacted documents, surveillance frames, case files, ritual diagrams.

### Neon Street Mythology
Graffiti annotations, surveillance footage, fashion-editorial portraiture, transit maps.

### Children's Adventure
Sketchbook energy, stickers, maps, found objects, playful diagrams.

### Prestige Space Opera
Mission telemetry, cinematic vistas, engineering diagrams, diplomatic records.

### Historical Epic
Painted chronicles, archaeological reconstruction, material-culture studies.

The **narrative architecture stays WIND**.

The **skin belongs to the world**.

## WIND SPREAD TYPES

The skill should select the spread archetype that best advances development.

### ORIGIN SPREAD

Shows why a thing exists.

Best for:
- institutions,
- technologies,
- factions,
- traditions,
- relationships.

Question:
**What event made this necessary?**

---

### OPERATION SPREAD

Shows the subject performing its intended function.

Best for:
- characters,
- teams,
- weapons,
- occupations,
- vehicles,
- powers.

Question:
**What does competence look like here?**

---

### FAILURE SPREAD

Shows what happens when the system breaks.

Best for:
- revealing limitations,
- stakes,
- biology,
- infrastructure,
- power costs.

Question:
**What is this thing afraid of?**

---

### ORDINARY-LIFE SPREAD

Shows how the fantastic has become mundane.

Best for:
- making worlds inhabitable,
- culture,
- architecture,
- economy,
- social behavior.

Question:
**What doesn't impress the people who live here anymore?**

---

### ECONOMY SPREAD

Shows value moving between people.

Best for:
- resources,
- class,
- technology,
- trade,
- scarcity.

Question:
**What does everyone need, and who controls access?**

---

### POLITICAL SPREAD

Shows competing legitimate interests colliding.

Best for:
- factions,
- governments,
- succession,
- treaties,
- law.

Question:
**What happens when two reasonable parties cannot both get what they need?**

---

### RELATIONSHIP SPREAD

Shows two characters through their interaction rather than biographies.

Best for:
- protagonists,
- rivals,
- mentors,
- families,
- interspecies relationships.

Question:
**What can we learn about both characters that neither would tell us directly?**

---

### MYSTERY SPREAD

Shows evidence before explanation.

Best for:
- antagonists,
- anomalies,
- plot escalation,
- hidden history.

Question:
**What fact should be impossible?**

---

### CONSEQUENCE SPREAD

Shows the aftermath of a prior decision.

Best for:
- maintaining narrative continuity,
- demonstrating systemic causality.

Question:
**What changed because of what just happened?**

---

### REVEAL SPREAD

Overturns a previous assumption about the world.

Best for:
- new cultures,
- hidden factions,
- scale expansion,
- reinterpretation of history.

Question:
**What did the audience think was universal that is actually provincial?**

---

## CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT THROUGH WIND

When asked to WIND a character, do not immediately create a conventional character sheet.

Determine:

1. What does this person do better than most people?
2. What do they want in the scene?
3. What prevents them from getting it?
4. What decision reveals who they are?
5. What physical or cultural design details become necessary because of that decision?
6. What hidden contradiction should the image contain?

Then construct the scene.

Afterward extract:

- silhouette,
- clothing,
- tools,
- physiological traits,
- habits,
- affiliations,
- scars,
- abilities,
- vulnerabilities,
- relationship cues.

A later conventional model sheet may be produced from accumulated WIND canon if production requires one.

---

## FACTION DEVELOPMENT THROUGH WIND

Never introduce a faction with a lineup alone.

Show the faction:

- solving a problem,
- enforcing a rule,
- celebrating,
- mourning,
- negotiating,
- failing,
- punishing,
- rescuing,
- trading,
- or interacting with an opposing faction.

Supporting panels may reveal:

- hierarchy,
- visual language,
- resources,
- doctrine,
- internal contradictions,
- territory,
- allies,
- enemies,
- recruitment,
- equipment,
- economic base.

---

## CREATURE DEVELOPMENT THROUGH WIND

Treat creatures as organisms before monsters unless the project's style says otherwise.

Show:

- feeding,
- mating,
- migration,
- disease,
- parenting,
- territorial behavior,
- aging,
- predation,
- symbiosis,
- interaction with civilization.

Supporting panels may include:

- anatomy,
- scale,
- lifecycle,
- locomotion,
- senses,
- ecology,
- vulnerabilities,
- regional morphs.

Avoid “cool creature standing there” unless the stillness itself tells the story.

---

## POWER / TECHNOLOGY DEVELOPMENT THROUGH WIND

Never demonstrate only maximum capability.

Show:

**Input → mechanism → use → cost → failure → aftermath**

The resulting spread should answer:

- Why was this invented?
- Who can use it?
- Who cannot afford it?
- What does misuse look like?
- What infrastructure supports it?
- What happens afterward?

---

## CINEMATIC SEQUENCING

When creating multiple WIND spreads, do not treat them independently.

Build a visual narrative sequence.

A strong development run often follows:

**Premise → Function → Vulnerability → Cultural Contrast → Antagonistic Pressure → Ordinary Life → Economy → Governance → Field Operation → Emotional Relationship → Mystery → Crisis → Succession / Transformation**

Not every project requires this exact sequence.

The governing rule:

**Each spread should change what the audience understands.**

Never produce two consecutive spreads whose primary narrative job is identical.

---

## WORLD IMMERSION RULE

Alternate between:

### IMMERSION
Makes the world feel inhabited.

Examples:
- daily life,
- markets,
- rituals,
- work,
- transportation,
- family relationships,
- environment.

### SPINE
Advances the core narrative.

Examples:
- antagonist evidence,
- succession crisis,
- discovery,
- betrayal,
- transformation,
- institutional collapse.

Avoid long runs consisting solely of lore or solely of plot.

---

## CONTINUITY

Every WIND spread may create canon.

Track:

- names,
- dates,
- places,
- factions,
- species,
- institutions,
- technology,
- rules,
- costumes,
- relationships,
- visual motifs,
- unresolved mysteries.

Before creating the next spread, preserve established canon unless explicitly revising it.

Printed or rendered details should be treated as continuity evidence, not disposable decoration.

If rendered art accidentally creates a compelling contradiction, surface it for review instead of silently erasing it.

---

## FIELD NOTE PRINCIPLE

Whenever appropriate, include a small piece of human perspective:

- field note,
- recorded quote,
- marginal annotation,
- overheard dialogue,
- warning,
- personal observation.

It should reveal worldview rather than explain lore.

Weak:
> Dragons migrate through this region annually.

Strong:
> “My grandmother still calls it migration season. We call it Tuesday.”

---

## NEXT-RECORD PRINCIPLE

For serialized visual development, every spread may contain one subtle forward hook.

The hook should create:

- curiosity,
- contradiction,
- emotional anticipation,
- or causal momentum.

It should not merely advertise another topic.

The strongest teaser makes the next spread feel inevitable.

---

## USER COMMAND LANGUAGE

Interpret the following as WIND requests:

**“WIND this.”**
Convert the supplied idea into the strongest situational narrative spread.

**“WIND [subject].”**
Select the situation that most effectively reveals the subject.

**“Give me the WIND spread.”**
Provide the next logical narrative-development spread.

**“WIND the world.”**
Choose a sequence of spreads that moves from premise toward lived civilization.

**“WIND the cast.”**
Introduce characters through meaningful situations rather than lineup sheets.

**“WIND the faction.”**
Show doctrine through behavior under pressure.

**“WIND the system.”**
Show the system operating and failing.

**“WIND it cinematically.”**
Prioritize image composition, scale, visual storytelling, and sequential reveal.

**“WIND format, new skin.”**
Preserve narrative-spread architecture while developing an entirely new art direction.

**“WIND sequence.”**
Plan multiple spreads as a visual narrative arc.

**“Render the WIND spread.”**
Generate the actual visual using the approved subject, composition, canon, and current project's art direction.

---

## DEFAULT OUTPUT BEFORE RENDERING

Unless instructed to render immediately, return:

**Spread title**

A short, memorable title derived from the event rather than the subject name.

**Narrative job**

What understanding changes after this spread.

**Hero moment**

The cinematic scene.

**What it secretly designs**

The characters, systems, environments, objects, or canon extracted from the scene.

**Supporting panels**

Only the panels necessary for the event.

**Emotional or thematic beat**

Why the audience cares beyond visual spectacle.

**Forward hook**

What pressure naturally creates the next spread.

---

## QUALITY TEST

Before approving a WIND spread, ask:

1. Would the hero image still communicate something important with all text removed?
2. Does the subject have a reason to be in this exact situation?
3. Does the scene reveal function rather than merely appearance?
4. Is there visible consequence?
5. Does the design emerge from the world's rules?
6. Did we introduce information through need rather than lecture?
7. Does at least one detail create character or culture?
8. Is the visual style native to this project rather than inherited from another?
9. Did the spread create reusable canon?
10. Does it make the audience want to see what happens next?

If fewer than eight answers are yes, redesign the spread.

---

## PRIME DIRECTIVE

**Do not illustrate the lore.**

**Find the moment in which the lore becomes unavoidable, then photograph that moment as though the world were real.**
